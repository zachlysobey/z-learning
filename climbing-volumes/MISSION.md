# Mission: Climbing volumes

## Why
Build plywood climbing volumes for a home wall, learning real "handy" skills along the way:
measuring, cutting bevels, joining, finishing. If the results are good, volumes could become a
small side-hustle later, so the fundamentals should be learned properly rather than hacked.
There is existing material to put to use: wall panels bought from defunct gyms (candidate raw
stock for volumes) and two rough, unfinished plywood triangle volumes from Facebook Marketplace.

## Success looks like
- Can look at a volume (or a photo of one) and name its parts, its joint type, and how it mounts.
- Finishes the two Marketplace triangles: sanded, textured, painted, T-nutted, mounted, climbed on.
- Designs a simple volume from scratch: picks a shape, computes the bevel angles, writes a cut list.
- Builds one triangle volume from new or salvaged ply with a chosen, understood tool setup.
- Has a defensible answer to "can the gym panels be cut into volumes?" and, if yes, has done it.
- Knows what separates a home-wall volume from a sellable one (finish, hardware, standards).

## Constraints
- Very little woodworking so far; bevel and miter angles are new.
- Tools: drill and impact driver, basic hand tools, some T-nuts and hardware. No saw yet, and no
  appetite to buy tools until it is justified. Unfinished basement is the workspace.
- Hands-on bandwidth may be scarce for a while: lessons must build fundamentals that survive a gap,
  and early lessons should be doable with what is already owned.
- Small attainable milestones, but not rushing to a first output at the expense of understanding.

## Out of scope
- Building the home wall itself (framing, panels, T-nut grids); volumes only.
- Fiberglass or polyester-resin volumes; plywood only for now.
- Routesetting theory beyond what is needed to decide which volume shapes are worth building.
